In one week, I used AI to build an RFP tool that cut our response time from over a week to under 10 minutes and replaced $112,000 a year in outside software and consulting.

I'm Max Fihma, cofounder of Fihma Advisory. Before I helped anyone else put AI to work, I did it at my own desk. This is the story of how a boutique asset manager in NYC stopped paying for RFP help and started answering RFPs in minutes with a tool built in house.

The problem

In financial services, winning new business means answering RFPs (requests for proposal) and DDQs (due diligence questionnaires). These are long documents, often hundreds of questions, asking everything about how a firm invests, operates and manages risk.

A single RFP used to take us over a week. Most of that time wasn't spent thinking. It was spent searching: digging through old responses for the answer we'd already written a dozen times, copying it over, fixing the formatting, and checking that it was still accurate.

To keep up, we were paying for two things:

  • AutoRFP, an AI RFP software platform, at about $60,000 a year
  • An outside consultant who helped us fill these out, at about $52,000 a year

That's $112,000 a year, and RFPs still took days.

The turning point

I knew exactly how our RFP process worked and where the time went. So instead of paying for someone else's general purpose product, I built one shaped around how we actually work, using Claude Code, an AI tool that writes and fixes code.

It took me one week.

What the tool does

The tool is now built into our company's systems and available to the whole firm. Here's how it works:

  • It reads the document. Drop in an RFP and it parses out every question automatically.
  • It searches our answer base. We have thousands of approved questions and responses. For each new question, the tool searches by meaning, not just keywords, so it finds the right answer even when the question is worded differently.
  • It gives the top three options. For every question, it surfaces the three best matching answers so a person can pick the right one in seconds.
  • It can write a new response. When a question is genuinely new, there's an option to have AI draft an answer.
  • It keeps an audit trail. Every answer shows who added it, so there's always accountability.
  • It keeps answers fresh. An internal clock flags answers that need to be reviewed on a regular schedule, so nothing outdated goes out the door.
  • It puts everything back in the original format. The finished answers go back into the RFP exactly as the client sent it, ready to submit.

It wasn't perfect

The week wasn't smooth. A few problems almost stopped it:

  • Every RFP is formatted differently. Some come as Word documents with tables inside tables, some as spreadsheets with merged cells, some as PDFs. The first version missed questions hidden in tables and treated three part questions as one. I rebuilt the reader so it handles each format on its own terms.
  • Search matched words, not meaning. Early on, a question about "sustainability integration" wouldn't find our answer on "ESG policy," even though they ask the same thing. Switching to search by meaning, and showing the top three matches instead of one, fixed it.
  • Putting answers back broke the formatting. The first time I filled in a client's document, fonts changed and tables shifted. In a field where presentation matters, that's not acceptable. I reworked it so answers are written into the original file without touching anything else.

Each of these would have been a support ticket and a long wait with a software vendor. Building it myself meant I could fix them the same day.

What this means for your business

Most businesses are paying for software that does 80% of what they need and 100% of what they don't. They pay for outside help to fill the gaps. And they still lose hours to work that's mostly searching, copying and reformatting.

That's exactly the kind of work AI can take off your plate. A tool built around how your business actually runs can cost less than what you're paying today and do the job better. You don't need to be technical to get there. That's the part we handle.
